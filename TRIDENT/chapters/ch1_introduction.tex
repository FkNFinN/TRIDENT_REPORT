\subsection{Noise Pollution in UUV}

Big commercial submarines adopt another method providing more control, and almost no noise is created. A ballast tank, which is by all mean a better method for a lengthy period hovering, compared to other UUV. But this is hard to implement in a small-scale submersible.

\subsection{Enhancing Marine Species Monitoring in Thailand}

Monitoring pollution levels and marine biodiversity is crucial for managing
ecosystems sustainably. As markers of the health of the environment, marine species' loss frequently indicates more serious problems with habitat degradation, temperature changes, or water quality.

However, traditional maritime monitoring techniques that are commonly
used in Thailand, such as diver-based surveys or equipment installed on boats, are risky, time-consuming, and labor-intensive. These techniques are further constrained by depth limitations, strong currents, and low vision.

\subsection{Ensuring Maintenance of Underwater Systems}

Underwater systems, for example, pipelines, fish farms, and marine research
equipment, are constantly exposed to harsh environmental conditions including corrosion, and damage from marine life. These systems require regular inspection, repair, and maintenance to remain functional and safe.


Traditional inspection methods rely on divers or expensive Remotely
Operated Vehicles (ROVs), which are costly, time-consuming, and pose safety risks especially in deep or murky waters. As a result, many installations suffer from delayed maintenance and undetected failures, leading to higher repair costs or system downtime.

\subsection{Traditional UUV is costly}

Unmanned Underwater Vehicles are essential for tasks such as marine
research, environmental monitoring, and infrastructure inspection. However, most traditional UUVs are expensive and inaccessible to small research labs, universities, and local conservation groups.For instance, Chasing gladius Mini (35,000 –60,000 Thai baht), BlueROV2 (180,000 Thai baht), and VideoRay Pro 5 (500,000 – 3,700,000 Thai baht). These systems also require additional costs for operation, deployment, and training. Due to these barriers, many underwater systems go uninspected, and marine research is limited—especially in developing regions like Thailand.


\section{OBJECTIVES OF THE PROJECT}

The primary objective of this project is to design and develop multipurpose Unmanned Underwater Vehicle (UUV) that supports various underwater applications in both research and maintenance. The system is aimed to provide affordable, modular, and adaptable platform that can operate effectively in canal and coastal environments of Thailand. 
 \textbf{Key Objectives}
\begin{enumerate}
	\item Develop a Modular Multi-Use UUV Platform:	
	
	Create a compact and cost-effective UUV structure that allows easy 
	integration of various tools and sensors based on mission requirements. 
	Design the system to support plug-and-play modules for quick 
	reconfiguration.
	\item Enable Underwater Debris Detection: 
	
	Integrate vision-based detection for identifying marine litter and 
	seabed debris. Attach basic cleaning tools or manipulators for collecting or tagging debris for environmental maintenance.
	\item Support Marine Biological Research (Ichthyology):
	
	Equip the UUV with high-resolution cameras to monitor marine 
	life habitats and behaviors. Enable data collection for research in 
	ichthyology and marine biodiversity mapping.
	\item Monitor and Inspect Underwater Infrastructure:
	
	Facilitate routine inspection of underwater systems such as pipelines, 
	fish cages, research sensors, and marine installations. Provide 
	real-time visual data for structural assessment and potential failure 
	detection. 
	\item Reducing Noise Pollution in UUV:
	
	Reduce the noise pollution created by other modern UUV, by reverting to 
	a traditional mean. A fully controllable ballast tank and maneuverable 
	hovering. 
\end{enumerate}

\section{SCOPE AND LIMITATIONS OF PROJECT}

To minimize manufacturing costs while maintaining structural 
functionality, the connecting components of the Unmanned Underwater 
Vehicle (UUV) were fabricated by 3D-printing PETG components before 
applying a resin shell around. This method allows for rapid prototyping, 
ease of customization, and acceptable water resistance, particularly in 
shallow-to-moderate depth applications. Traditional 3D-printed materials 
such as PLA, while accessible, exhibit poor waterproofing and dimensional 
stability in underwater environments, making them unsuitable for critical 
structural components.

Despite its advantages, PETG with resin coat exhibits lower mechanical 
properties. Specifically, tensile strength, impact resistance, and 
fatigue tolerance when compared to industrial-grade polymers (e.g., 
polycarbonate, PEEK) or metallic materials. These limitations must be 
considered in operational contexts involving hydrostatic pressures, 
mechanical vibrations, or potential collision events. Finite Element 
Analysis (FEA) is recommended for evaluating stress concentrations within 
these components under load conditions.

Given the spatial constraints inherent in the UUV’s compact form factor, 
the onboard battery modules are limited in both physical size and 
capacity, leading to a reduction in operational endurance. This imposes 
constraints on mission duration and necessitates efficient power 
management strategies, such as pulse-width modulation (PWM) for thruster 
control and low-power sleep modes for auxiliary systems. Trade-offs 
between vehicle autonomy and power density are actively evaluated.

For underwater communication, Bluetooth and other RF-based protocols are 
infeasible due to rapid signal attenuation in conductive media like 
seawater. To overcome this, the UUV employs a tethered Ethernet-based 
Local Area Network (LAN) for high-throughput, low-latency communication d
uring deployment and testing. This configuration supports real-time 
control, telemetry acquisition, and bulk data transfer, which are 
critical for debugging, mission uploads, and sensor data retrieval.

The vehicle is equipped with two ballast tanks designed to modulate its 
net buoyancy, allowing for depth control without constant thrust input. 
By achieving near-neutral buoyancy and increasing vertical displacement, 
the UUV can maintain submerged states for extended periods. This feature 
is advantageous for the UUV’s primary objective: Reducing Noise Pollution 
in UUV. Reduced propulsion noise and acoustic signal created by other UUV. 

\section{Methodology Used in the Project}

TRIDENT is designed to have a highly compact form factor, as its 
structure contains air, which increases buoyancy and makes submersion 
more challenging due to density differences. Since one of the primary 
objectives of the project is to navigate confined spaces, minimizing the 
vehicle's size enhances its ability to operate efficiently in such 
environments. 

\subsection{Software Development}

The robot primarily operates using ROS (Robot Operating System), which 
provides a flexible framework for handling navigation, sensor integration, 
and autonomous decision-making. For underwater mapping, the robot employs 
Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM), a technique that allows it 
to construct a map of the seabed while simultaneously determining its own 
position within that environment. To achieve this, the system utilizes 
laser distance sensors, which play a crucial role in detecting the 
contours and features of the seabed in real time. These sensors, 
specifically infrared (IR) distance sensors from Sharp, are designed to 
measure distances within a range of 20 cm to 150 cm. They operate based 
on triangulation and reflected infrared light intensity, where the 
emitted IR beam is reflected off surfaces and detected by an internal 
position-sensitive detector (PSD). This data is then processed to 
estimate distances accurately, enabling the robot to create a detailed 
and precise underwater map while navigating autonomously.

\subsection{Communications}

The TRIDENT robot utilizes LAN communication for underwater operations, 
as water can completely block the radio frequency signals required for 
other forms of communication. Within the robot, a Raspberry Pi serves as 
the primary control unit, running ROS (Robot Operating System) to manage 
and facilitate communication with an Arduino Nano. The Arduino Nano is 
responsible for controlling the sensors and motors via UART (Universal 
Asynchronous Receiver-Transmitter). UART is a serial communication 
protocol that enables data transfer between devices without the need for 
a shared clock signal. Data is transmitted one bit at a time, including 
a start bit, data bits, an optional parity bit, and a stop bit. This 
protocol is commonly used in embedded systems, microcontrollers, and 
computers, supporting full-duplex communication through transmit (TX) 
and receive (RX) lines.

\section{Project Implementation Plan}

\subsection{Independent Site Selection and Mission Planning}

Site selection will be conducted independently based on publicly 
available data, prior studies, and observed environmental needs. Emphasis 
will be placed on identifying locations suitable for underwater 
inspection, ecological observation, and experimental data collection. 

\subsection{Data acquisition and analysis}

The UUV would deploy to conduct comprehensive seabed surveys. Generate 
detailed monitoring of the underwater terrain, accurately documenting the 
distribution of pollutants and the characteristics of marine habitats. 

\subsection{Targeted Debris Removal Operations}

UUV would deploy for targeted debris removal missions. The operations 
will focus on extracting pollutants, particularly plastics and abandoned 
fishing gear, from identified hotspots, thereby mitigating their adverse 
environmental impact.

\subsection{Marine Species Documentation and Monitoring}

The UUV will be employed to capture video and image data of marine 
species within their natural habitats. Which crucial for biodiversity 
assessments, species identification, and behavioral studies, contributing 
to a deeper understanding of the local marine ecosystems. 

\subsection{Underwater Infrastructure Inspection}

Routine inspections of underwater infrastructure, including pipelines, 
fish cages, and other installations. These inspections will facilitate 
the detection of structural damage, corrosion, and other potential issues, 
enabling timely maintenance and preventing costly failures.

\section{Benefits Derived from the Research}

\subsection{Reduce Seabed Pollution}

The UUV's advanced mapping and debris removal capabilities will directly 
contribute to cleaner and healthier marine environments. By accurately 
identifying and targeting pollution hotspots, particularly those 
contaminated with plastic and abandoned fishing gear, the project will 
mitigate the detrimental effects of these pollutants on marine life.

\subsection{Data-Driven Conservation}

The UUV will provide data that supports informed decision-making in 
marine conservation and management. This data will enable targeted 
interventions and enhance the effectiveness of conservation efforts.

\subsection{Improved Underwater Infrastructure Maintenance}

By deploying the UUV for routine inspections of underwater 
infrastructure, such as pipelines and fish cages, the project will 
reduce the reliance on expensive and potentially hazardous diver-based 
inspections. Increasing driver safety.

\subsection{Design of a Device}

For a modular use UUV platform, the robot must be compatible with a tool 
such as robot arm for collecting debris or holding on to pipeline. For 
inspection and monitoring, the device is equipped with a camera module, 
two hydrostatic pressure sensors and two laser distant sensors. These 
sensors enable the use of Point Cloud for mapping the underwater bed for 
more data collecting.

Additionally, focusing primarily on minimizing environmental impact, the 
design of our robot is centered around this concept. Two key improvements 
over traditional UUVs are (1) the elimination of noise pollution 
generated by the motor and (2) reducing the cost of the robot by 
optimizing its design. Additionally, the robot is designed to have a 
smooth, rounded shape with as few openings as possible to better 
withstand the challenges of Thailand’s polluted waterways.
